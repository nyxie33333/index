02 / THE BIG PICTURE

VR has potential. Readiness matters.

Virtual reality (VR) uses computer-generated environments that allow users to explore and interact with simulated spaces. In education, it can support virtual field trips, simulations and experiential learning.

TECHNOLOGY + PEOPLE
🥽TECHNOLOGY TREND

Virtual Reality

Imagine taking a class on a virtual visit to a zoo, rainforest or historical site. Learners can explore a simulated environment instead of only reading about it.

🔐KEY ISSUE

Data security & confidentiality

VR platforms may collect account details, voice, images, movement, interaction records or other user information. Schools must know what is collected, why and who can access it.

🛰️CHALLENGE 01

Technological infrastructure

Schools may lack enough headsets, compatible devices, charging facilities, technical support or reliable internet access.

🧑‍🏫CHALLENGE 02

Teacher readiness

Teachers need practical training, confidence and lesson-design skills to connect VR experiences to clear learning outcomes.

🌍LEARNING OPPORTUNITY

Virtual field trips

A virtual zoo tour can help pupils observe animal habitats, compare features and discuss conservation without travelling to the physical location.

🧩DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Purpose before technology

Choose VR only when it adds learning value. A headset is not automatically better than a well-designed video, image or group discussion.

Evidence snapshot: A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis examined 52 empirical studies on VR in teacher education and reported a positive, moderate overall effect. The review also stresses that outcomes vary by equipment, immersion level and learning objectives (Han et al., 2025).
03 / SAFE BY DESIGN

Who is watching the virtual world?

A VR experience can feel like a private adventure, but digital systems may process information behind the scenes. Data protection must be part of lesson planning, not an afterthought.

PRIVACY FIRST

🔎 What data could be involved?

  • Identity data: name, email address, username or school account.
  • Audio-visual data: voice recordings, photographs or video, if enabled.
  • Interaction data: clicks, choices, activity history and session duration.
  • Device or movement data: device identifiers, location or motion-related data, depending on the platform and permissions.

The exact data collected depends on the device, application, settings and provider. Always check the platform's privacy notice.

🛡️ The SAFE VR protocol

S · Select A · Ask F · Filter E · Evaluate

  • Select school-approved tools with clear privacy information.
  • Ask for appropriate consent and explain the activity to learners and parents or guardians where required.
  • Filter unnecessary permissions, personal details and public sharing features.
  • Evaluate data retention, account deletion, access controls and provider practices.
Use the minimum data necessary. Do not upload pupils’ names, faces, voices or identifiable information to an unapproved platform.

04 / MAKING IT POSSIBLE

Two barriers. Practical solutions.

Effective integration is not about buying the most expensive equipment. It is about matching available resources, teacher capacity and lesson goals.

PLAN FOR REAL SCHOOLS

🛰️ Infrastructure solutions

  • Start small: pilot one short VR activity before scaling up.
  • Shared access: use station rotation or small groups when headsets are limited.
  • Low-tech alternative: offer a 360° video, desktop simulation, projected tour or image sequence.
  • Connectivity: check bandwidth and download permitted offline content in advance.
  • Maintenance: plan charging, cleaning, updates, storage and technical support.

🧑‍🏫 Teacher-readiness solutions

  • Hands-on training: practise device setup, navigation and troubleshooting.
  • Pedagogical planning: align the VR task with curriculum outcomes and assessment.
  • Guided exploration: provide a clear mission, prompts and reflection questions.
  • Peer support: co-plan with colleagues and share tested lesson ideas.
  • Evaluate: collect learner feedback and review whether VR improved learning.
Inclusive rule: every learner should have an equivalent way to achieve the learning objective, whether or not they can or wish to use a headset.

DESIGN A MINI LESSON

From experience to evidence.

Use this simple sequence to make the virtual zoo purposeful:

01
Before the tour

Introduce habitat vocabulary and explain headset safety, privacy and movement boundaries.

02
During the tour

Observe two animals. Record habitat features using a worksheet without unnecessary personal data.

03
After the tour

Compare habitats in pairs and explain one adaptation using evidence from observations.

Assessment evidence: completed observation sheet, pair discussion and a short exit ticket.

06 / TEACHER TOOLKIT

Plan a VR lesson that works.

A practical decision guide for teachers before introducing an immersive activity.

READY · SAFE · PURPOSEFUL
🎯

1. Learning purpose

Write one measurable objective. Identify what pupils should know or do after the VR activity.

🧰

2. Resource check

Check devices, compatibility, charging, connectivity, accessibility and a backup plan.

🔏

3. Privacy check

Review permissions, accounts, consent, data collection, storage and deletion practices.

🧑‍🏫

4. Teacher rehearsal

Test the activity first. Prepare instructions, transitions, support and troubleshooting steps.

♿

5. Inclusive options

Offer a desktop, projected, captioned or non-headset alternative with equivalent learning outcomes.

📊

6. Evaluate impact

Check pupil understanding, participation, comfort and whether the technology added value.

LIVE LEARNING PLAN

🎥 Session overview

Title: VR in the Classroom — Exciting Learning, Safe Data

Target audience: Pre-service and in-service teachers

Platform: Google Meet / YouTube Live (select one according to access and privacy requirements)

Purpose: Help teachers evaluate how VR can be used meaningfully and safely despite infrastructure and readiness constraints.

Engagement tools: Live chat, polls, scenario prompts and Q&A.

Do not display pupil names, personal records or private classroom footage during the stream. Obtain permission before showing any identifiable participant.

🗂️ Live-stream content and flow

01
Welcome and introduction

Introduce the topic and explain that participants will explore meaningful and safe classroom VR use.

02
Opening poll: Would you use VR in your classroom?

Invite viewers to vote yes, not yet or unsure, then briefly discuss their reasons.

03
VR explained through a virtual zoo

Demonstrate how a virtual tour can support observation, vocabulary and discussion.

04
Privacy and confidentiality alert

Explain possible data collection, platform permissions, consent, account protection and safe sharing.

05
Infrastructure challenge

Discuss limited headsets, compatible devices, internet access and practical low-cost alternatives.

06
Teacher readiness challenge

Highlight hands-on training, lesson alignment, troubleshooting and peer support.

07
Audience scenario and discussion

Ask viewers to suggest a safe plan for a class with limited headsets and unstable internet.

08
Key takeaways and Q&A

Summarise the SAFE VR protocol and invite questions before closing the session.

💬 Suggested audience questions

  • What information might a VR application collect from its users?
  • How can teachers include pupils when there are not enough headsets?
  • What training would help teachers feel confident using VR?
  • When would a normal video or classroom activity be more suitable than VR?
09 / DIGITAL CITIZENSHIP

Ethical use is part of good teaching.

Use digital tools responsibly and model the behaviour expected from learners.

BE SAFE · BE FAIR · GIVE CREDIT
©️

Copyright

Use original, licensed or openly licensed media. Check the licence and conditions before downloading, adapting or sharing.

📝

Academic integrity

Submit original work, acknowledge ideas and quotations, and do not present another person's work as your own.

🔒

Data privacy

Collect the minimum data needed, use approved platforms and protect learner accounts and records.

🤖

Responsible AI use

Use AI to support brainstorming or drafting, verify its output, disclose use when required and never enter confidential pupil data.

🔗

Source attribution

Credit authors, organisations, images, videos, software and AI tools. Include working links and access details.

🧠

Wellbeing and choice

Provide breaks, clear physical boundaries and an alternative for learners who experience discomfort or prefer not to use a headset.
